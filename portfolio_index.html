Case 01 / 04

Cordelia ARC — supportive-living platform, public site to admin back office

A California supportive-living agency serving adults with intellectual disabilities across Alameda and Contra Costa counties. What began as a marketing site became the software the agency runs on — referrals, hiring, compliance, and billing.

The same referral and intake operations I usually automate inside a CRM — this time built as an actual application.
Sector
Healthcare · HIPAA-regulated
Stack
React · TypeScript · Supabase
Scale

Surface
40+ routed pages

Problem

The agency ran on disconnected parts: a brochure website, paper onboarding packets, referrals tracked by hand, and compliance documents that had to be printed, signed, and scanned back. Nothing talked to anything else, and every new hire meant re-keying the same details into a dozen forms.

Approach

  • Built the public site and the internal platform as one React + TypeScript application, so marketing, referrals, and operations share a data layer.
  • Put every privileged action behind role-based access enforced in Postgres, not merely hidden in the UI.
  • Moved the paper packet into a browser flow — PDF form-filling and e-signature, with phone OTP verification.
  • Pushed integration work into Supabase edge functions so the front-end never holds a third-party secret.

Outcome

  • 91 database migrations and ~50 edge functions in production.
  • 40+ routed pages spanning the public site, a compliance portal, and the admin back office.
  • Referrals, HR, compliance, and billing run in one system instead of four.
  • Onboarding is a link, not a packet.

Case 02 / 04

Kemp Beauty — the email problem nobody could see

A DTC lash brand with eight live Klaviyo flows and a storefront that needed real product-page work. The flows all showed green in the dashboard. Almost none of the email was reaching anyone.

All eight flows showed live. None of them were being delivered.
Sector
DTC beauty · Shopify
Scope
Deliverability · theme dev
Fixed
26 messages across 8 flows
Root cause
DMARC p=reject, unsigned domain
Visit store ↗
Open the full recordClose the record ›

Problem

Every marketing email was sent from info@kempbeauty.com — a domain with no DKIM keys in Klaviyo and a DMARC policy of p=reject. Gmail refused all of it:

550 5.7.26 Unauthenticated email … not accepted due to DMARC policy

Klaviyo showed all eight flows as live the entire time. Flow status and actual delivery are two different things, and nothing in the interface surfaced the gap.

Approach

  • Audited sending domains and found exactly one verified: mail.kempbeauty.com, DKIM and site-verification records confirmed.
  • Tested authenticating the bare root domain and established it is impossible, not merely awkward — CNAME records cannot exist at a zone apex.
  • Rewrote the sender across 26 messages in 8 flows, including a live A/B test's variations, keeping reply-to on the real inbox.
  • Traced a second, separate failure: double opt-in confirmation emails carry their own sender setting that Klaviyo's API doesn't expose — invisible until you test a real signup.

Outcome

  • Verified with a live signup end to end: submit → subscribe → delivered to Gmail, zero bounces or drops.
  • Cross-checked against Klaviyo's own reporting rather than trusting the event log alone.
  • Flagged the tradeoff rather than burying it: the mail. subdomain's MX points at Klaviyo, so replies there never reach the team.
  • Recommended staged domain warming instead of resuming full volume on a subdomain with no sending history.

Problem

The business sells consulting, not products — but the site runs on Shopify. Every service page, resource, and podcast episode needed a structure the platform doesn't ship with, and the episode pages in particular had to work as genuine editorial content rather than product listings.

Approach

  • A long tail of real mobile fixes — breadcrumb overflow, stacking icon rows, list markers breaking onto their own line, video ordering in step cards.
  • Changes tracked in Git against a live theme, so every fix is reviewable and reversible.

Workflow samples

Inside the automations

Real builds pulled straight from GoHighLevel and Zapier — the actual trigger, the logic branching, and where the data ends up. Click any screenshot to enlarge.

01 / 04GoHighLevel

EDC time-slot form → shuttle & hotel routing → Google Sheets

Guests submit a form with their arrival time slot. The workflow triggers on submission, tags the contact, then checks the notes field to work out which shuttle and hotel package applies — and writes the result into the ops Google Sheet so the ground team has it without asking.

02 / 04Zapier

Multi-venue event booking → GoHighLevel contact sync

A new booking arrives from the events calendar and immediately splits into six paths — one per venue across the Vegas and Miami locations. Each path creates or updates the right contact in GoHighLevel with venue-specific tags, so reporting stays clean per property.

03 / 04Zapier

WooCommerce order → shuttle or hotel opportunity

Fires the moment a customer buys a shuttle or hotel package on WooCommerce. The order splits into two paths based on what was purchased, and each one opens the matching opportunity in the CRM pipeline — so sales sees the deal without anyone re-entering it.

04 / 04Zapier + AI

Inbox monitoring → AI affiliate detection → CRM update

Watches a Gmail inbox and splits incoming mail on whether the subject line mentions an affiliate. Affiliate email gets passed to an AI step that reads the message and extracts the details, which then update the contact record in the CRM automatically.
